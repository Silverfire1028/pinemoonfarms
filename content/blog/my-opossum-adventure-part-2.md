## [My Opossum Adventure Part 2](https://hotmesstohomestead.wordpress.com/2014/10/15/my-opossum-adventure-part-2/)

I get him into the house and turn on the kitchen light. All this time, he has sat in the crook of my arm, not making a sound and not moving. I thought he was dying, at this point. As I place him on the counter, I realize it is one of my sister-in-law's chickens. Because *o**f course it was.* 

As I look him over, I don't see anything out of place. As in, he still has two wings, two feet, and a head. I don't see any blood. I sighed in relief. At least I don't have to play vet at 1 am. I started looking closer. He had several tail feathers pulled out, and a few scratches on his back. None of them were bleeding. I took him to the bathroom to wash his cuts and check him out further.

There is no counter space in my bathroom, so I put him in the sink. He just sits there, all calm like. At this point I think he is in shock. I really don't want to lose him to shock. This already happened once, to the same chicken.  He was attacked when he was very young, and my sister-in-law nursed him back to health.

But the longer I looked, the less in shock he seemed. He was alert, looking around, making chicken noises, and otherwise acting normal. Except being very calm about being in a bathroom sink. Huh.

I clean out the cuts, make sure he is still not bleeding or anything is broken, and wrap him in a towel. I carry him outside and put him in the coop.

I counted the chickens. Five. Six, including the one I just doctored. Six chickens. I own15.

So now I have to scour the area for wayward chickens and put them back where they belong. And of course all the ones that are out don't like people. And *of course* it is raining. It takes me an hour to track all of them down. I am climbing over wood stacks and through brier bushes to get my girls, because I really don't want to have to do this again. I don't know if that was the only opossum or not.

So an hour and a half later, I am soaked to the skin and covered in burrs and scrapes. But all my girls are put back where they belong. I check on them one last time, do one final count. I have 15 in the coop. I make my way to the house, peel of my wet cloths, get dry, and get back in the bed.

Fifteen minutes later I am back out in the rain.

I forgot to close the barn door.
