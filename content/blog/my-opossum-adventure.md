## [My Opossum Adventure](https://hotmesstohomestead.wordpress.com/2014/10/15/my-opossum-adventure/)

It's 1am, and I am laying all snuggled in my bed, when I hear a strange sound coming from outside. I mute my T.V, and listen. It sounds like an animal crying in pain. My first thought was the horses. We have a horse here, Moon, who REALLY dislikes dogs. I have seen her go after a dog and attack it until it decides it's no longer fun to chase. Our neighbor's dog (who is a tiny terror, er…. Terrier) just doesn't take the hint, and keeps chasing the horses until Moon runs him out of the field. I thought that this time she caught him.

The more I listened, however, I realized that it wasn't a dog whine. It took me about 15 seconds to realize that the sound was a chicken. Oh, no… Something is in my chicken pen.

I quickly got up and put on a heaver pair of pants (it was chilly last night!) and called my dog and went down the stairs.

Outside, the sound was plain. It was definitely a chicken. I took a few steps before I realized I was getting ready to interrupt a predator in a hunt. Not the smartest idea… We have coyote and feral dogs that don't like it when you mess with them. So I went back inside and grabbed my .22 rifle. It's barely big enough to be called a gun, but it does well enough for varmint control. I was hoping that if it was a coyote, I could at least scare it off.

Back out I go, and this time it is raining. Great. As I look out, I see my chickens scatter. Something has them seriously spooked. I walk to to the pen, and stay to the outside. Last thing I want is to be trapped close quarters with an angry…. whatever it was.

I look through the fence, and I see a chicken and a pair of eyes. I take aim and fire, knowing I was going to miss. The eyes retreated, leaving a very scared chicken. Somehow, he was outside the pen.

I go through the barn to get to where he was. My flashlight spots eyes near him again. An Opossum is waddling toward the chicken again. *Sigh.* This time, I know I can hit it. I don't really want to, but it's either that or my chicken. And possibly more, once he knows were there is easy food.

So I take care of the Opossum. I find my chicken huddled up next to the fence, scared out of his mind. I gather him up and tuck him next to my chest. I want to keep him immobilized until I can check him over. I don't feel or smell any blood, but that doesn't mean there isn't any. Scared of what I will find in the light, I hightail it back to the house, chicken in arm.
