Oh, boy. We are down to the final days. On Monday, I go to the hatchery and pick up our new chickens. There is quite the assortment coming home, and we are busy putting together the new brooder and getting everything set up.

Yesterday, we got the panels and the top finished, and the walls put together. It fits snuggly beside the coal stove on the hearth. At 4ftx4ft, this brooder is probably the nicest we've used, and we have used a bunch of DIY specials for a brooder box. Today, we went and bought feed and bedding, and tomorrow I will be finishing the brooder hot plates and setting everything up. We try not to use heat bulbs, as I find that this creates stress in the babies in the early days. They need 12ish hours of dark, and the bulb keeps them active for longer periods than they should be, in my opinion. And you can't turn it off, because then the babies will freeze. They make commercially made heat plates for brooders, but they are cost prohibitive and are open all around, not providing a dark environment for the chicks to rest in and feel protected.

I am super excited for this group of chicks, as this is the start of my breeding program, hopefully. They are layers of all colors of eggs, in demand breeds, and bases for crosses that I think would be in demand. I bought I rooster for each breeding group. Blue feathering features prominently in this group, because I LOVE the blue feather coloring on just about any breed. I will be breeding rose comb Olive Eggers, Sunshine Easter Eggers (bold colors), Sky Eggers (blue shades), Earthen Eggers (all shades of brown), Sparrow Eggers (spotted all colors), Flora Eggers (green shades), and many more. We will also offer pure bred chicks and hatching eggs for some, if not most of, the breeds we have.

Next spring is sure to bring some exciting times to the farm.
