## [Woke Up Sick, Reality Of Being a Farmer](https://hotmesstohomestead.wordpress.com/2024/07/30/woke-up-sick-reality-of-being-a-farmer/)

I love mornings. I really do. Except when I wake up vomiting because my body decided today was a good day to be a jerk. I had to take a cocktail of medications just to be able to go out and do chores this morning. I have a emesis bag next to me, just in case. Today is a fasting day, because solids will probably set off an attack, which could last several days. That's several days of being too nauseated to move, of constant meds, of no food and rationed liquids.

Despite how I feel, things *have* to be done. Some my husband can do. Others, I have to do, because he is at work. No matter how you feel, the animals need tended, things need picked, and chores need done. *No matter what.*

You don't get a day off being a farmer. This is not a 9-5 job. There's no such thing as a day off. There is no vacation. No break. No weekends. Things can be put off, but you just have to do double tomorrow. And some thing can't be put off, like feeding and watering. Not many farm sitters exist, and you are taking a chance every time you use one.

If you have animals, you now have to schedule around said animals. Animals do not take a break from eating and drinking (and making milk if you have a milking animal). Animals do not care that you are sick, they need taken care of. Bessy the cow doesn't care you are having fun at the party; she needs milked at 6pm. Late dinner? Not today! The chickens roost at dark, and the racoons will get in the coop if you don't close it (auto doors come with their own problems). This goes double if you have baby animals. I do not leave my house for a few weeks if I have chicks. Not even to go to the store. Things can go wrong so fast. Livestock will turn into deadstock at an alarming rate.

Farming isn't the glitz and glam you see on the internet. “Farmhouse Chic” doesn't exist out here. That is a lie that sells well, so it remains at the fore front of the internet. Farming is a dirty, smelly, 24/7 job that rarely pays well, if at all. There is never a break. It can be heartbreaking, maddening, hard, dirty, and mostly thankless.

So why do it?

Because you get the satisfaction that you can take care of your family if times get hard. Of growing your own food, knowing what is in it. Of making a living with your own hands. You get chickens that follow you like your the Pied Piper, and cluck is expectation of a treat. You get knowledge few have. You get to live in clean air and sunshine. You get a cow that acts like a 1200 pound dog when you scratch it's ears. You get experiences that many want, but will never have. You get to live with nature and all she offers.

Farmland is quickly disappearing to urban sprawl. Nature is giving way to suburbs. and with it small, family farms. The food supply chain now relies on mega farms and mass produced meat, eggs, and milk. This is dangerous, as we saw during Covid. A small disruption in the food distribution system can cause stores to empty frighteningly fast.

Being able to rely on yourself to feed your family is a gift few will ever receive.
