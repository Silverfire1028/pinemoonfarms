## [Something to Consider…](https://hotmesstohomestead.wordpress.com/2015/02/14/something-to-consider/)

If you are reading this, I assume you are indoors. Probably a safe assumption, considering it's the dead of winter. If you're like most people, you are probably sitting in a house. Or perhaps an apartment. Or a trailer. Wherever you are, it more than likely has four walls and a roof, yes?

Have you ever stopped to think about that?

Not, “Oh, yeah. I live in a house. So do a lot of people. So what?” But actually stopped to consider just what that means.

That the only thing between you, the elements, that storm raging outside or that tornado spinning past, is 6″ of wood? That the only thing holding it together are spikes of steel? Of the trees that were cut and milled, shipped, stacked, then measured and cut again, to be put in place by human hands? Of the crew of workers that didn't know you, have never met you, but built the very thing that keeps you safe and warm? The thing where you make memories, where you live?

For example, consider a window. Or your front door. What, exactly, do you think holds it in place? Do you know? I can tell you. A few screws/nails. That's it. The door and/or window more than likely came with it's own frame. That frame was placed in a rough sized hole, and then nailed in place. But you can't put very many, because it is ridiculously easy to pull it out of square. So you put a few, mainly where you put the shims (small pieces of wood that help square up the door) so that you can tighten them up without ruining the door. I can tell you exactly how many nails are holding in my back door.

Eight. Eight nails. That's it.

The only reason you don't notice? The door trim. The trim, that decorative strip of wood around the door? That thing you never notice unless it's broke or an ugly color? Yeah, that is vitally important. It hides the gap between the door and the hole it was put in. It helps holds the door in place. It keeps the drafts down, keeping your house warmer. If that small, 3″ wide strip wasn't installed correctly, you can have a whole host of problems. From drafts, to ugliness, to poor support. That thing you never notice is very important to your comfort.

How about your floor? You know what holds it up? Boards that are nailed to another with just a few nails. Mine have three in each end. Now, these boards are thick. I could drive a truck up one of those. So they are not going to break. But the only thing holding them from falling on the ground are 6 nails. Six. On top of that is a piece of plywood less than an inch thick. This is what supports you, your couch, TV, fridge, pets, and everything in your house. Same for the roof.

Now, why doesn't it all fall in at the slightest wind? Because of the way it is put together. Each board, every post, beam, rafter, perling, stud, and nailer, relays on one another. The entire structure is put together in such a way that the stress of the wind, snow, or just itself, is spread over a large area and doesn't put undo stress on just one part. One board never holds all the weight. One rafter, the entire snow load. One stud, the entire weight of the wall. Your entire house is put together in such a way that these 2 inch thick boards can support thousands of pounds, a storm, and high winds, all at the same time, and never complains.

Support. This is the thing that keeps you safe, warm, and comforted.

Neat how that works, isn't it?
