## [What did we get ourselves into?](https://hotmesstohomestead.wordpress.com/2014/10/07/what-did-we-get-ourselves-into/)

When my father came home and said he might have found a piece of land, I was ecstatic. We had been searching for over a year for a stretch of land and a seller willing to sell on land contract. Finally, we had a potentional place. It was 20 acres, with two barns and a creek on it. Better, it was in our price range. A dream property, right? I was so wrong. Nothing is that easy.

[![img](https://hotmesstohomestead.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/538974_615330045179152_1170760749_n-1.jpg?w=300)](https://hotmesstohomestead.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/538974_615330045179152_1170760749_n-1.jpg)The front of our future house

We went and looked at it, in the dead of winter, when the plant life was dead and the ground frozen solid. We could tell it was hilly (more of a valley, really) and the fences were basically rusted and broken woven wire, virtually nonexistent. The barn… Well, it was standing, at least. It needed cleaned, badly. Junk was scattered everywhere. The second barn wasn't much better, but it was more structurally sound than the bank barn, although filled with scrap, old wood, trash, feed bags, trash, broken glass, and trash. Did I mention the trash? There was a lot of it.

[![img](https://hotmesstohomestead.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/548284_615330061845817_621867520_n-1.jpg?w=300)](https://hotmesstohomestead.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/548284_615330061845817_621867520_n-1.jpg)The inside of the house

I walked to the edge of a hill and squatted down, observing and imagining what it would look like in summer, when the plants would paint the hills in green and the trees would be more than dark stick figures dotting the landscape.

My father came up behind me, and stood for a moment. “So, what do you think?” This was the moment I had to sway him either way, for he was on the fence and undecided if he wanted to buy something that would take so much work to set right. I sat there for a moment, taking it all in, picturing what it would look like after everything was fixed and put back to it's former glory. I stood up, dusting the snow off the knees of my Carhart's.

“It will do.”
