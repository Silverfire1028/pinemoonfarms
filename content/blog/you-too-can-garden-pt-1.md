## [You, too, can garden! pt. 1](https://hotmesstohomestead.wordpress.com/2016/05/10/you-too-can-garden-pt-1/)

I firmly believe that everyone can garden. Small back, front, or side yard? Small garden. Balcony in an apartment? Even smaller garden. Window? A few plants in pots. Home owners association won't allow a garden? Grow plants in your flowerbeds around the house. Get creative, get dirty, and get gardening!

First off, you need plants and some where to put them. Plants can be bought at large hardware stores, some big box stores, or your local greenhouse. But, I prefer to start mine myself, that way I know exactly what I'm planting. To start seedlings, you need small containers to start them in. Toilet paper tubes work, as do egg cartons, plastic cups, old Tupperware and other containers, and pots made from old newspaper.

The things to remember:

1. Will it hold up to repeated watering? Cardboard and multiple thicknesses of paper will stand up to water for the duration of growing. Most of these you will “direct plant” (plant right in the soil where you want your plant) instead of “up potting” (planting in a bigger pot to allow growth before moving to a final location).
2. Does it have anything toxic in it? I don't recommend anything that held anything even remotely toxic. Plastic packaging tends to absorb whatever is contained inside it. Even with repeated washing, the residue will not come out.
3. Does it drain well? Seedings are sensitive. Too little or too much water can kill them. You ideally want the soil moist, not sopping wet, and not dry to the touch.
4. Will it fit? Does your container fit on your windowsill, your table, or where ever your light is?

Alright. You are armed with your container. Now what? Dirt. That's what. This you will probably end up buying, unfortunately. Like I said before, seedlings are sensitive. While your adult plants can survive, and even thrive, in a varity of soils, seedings do better if the soil is loose, light, nutritionally balanced, and free of weed seeds. That's not to say you can't go and dig up dirt to plant in. But I don't really recommend it unless you have no other option.

You can buy seed starter in small bags that is perfect to start your seeds in. If you can't find it, regular potting soil can work, but don't expect an exelent germination rate.

Fill what ever container you are using with DAMP soil. By damp, I mean you should be able to form a ball, but when that ball is lightly bounced in the palm of your hand, it should break apart. You should fill the container to the top, without packing it down. You will do that when you plant.

Wait! You say. I don't have any seeds! Not to worry, you can get them usually the same place you bought your soil. Didn't buy soil? You can get seed packets at Wall-Mart, Meijer, Dollar General, Family Dollar, and a multitude of other stores. Decide what you want first before you go seed shopping.

Now, I understand a lot of people can't afford two and three dollar seed packs. I'm one of those. I'll let you in on a little not-so secret secret. Those $0.25 packets? I buy and grow a lot of those. While germination (rate of sprouting) can be spotty, if you are only looking for one or two plants, I feel this is the way to go.

Read the back of your packet. Does it say to plant a 1/4 inch deep? 1/8th? Barely cover? Follow the recommended depth. If it doesn't specify, a good rule of thumb is twice the width of the seed. Plant one or two per hole, and cover. LIGHTLY press down the soil. You don't want it compacted hard, but you need it a little pressed down. Lightly water, and set in your light source. Plant up to four times what you need. That way, you have at least a 1 in 4 chance of getting the number of plants that you need.

Wait! Don't throw the left over seeds away! Seeds can stay viable for several years. Close the packet, and store in a very cool, dry place. I store mine in the fridge, in a ziploc bag.

Now what? Water and wait. Water your seedlings every day, very lightly. Your goal is to keep the soil moist. A clean or new spray bottle works wonders for this, as the fine mist doesn't disturb the soil.

I think thats enough for now. In part two, I will explain how to take care of your seedlings, what true leaves are and why they are important, and what to do now that your a gardener!

My last tip to you is READ, READ, READ! Check out books from your local library, research on the internet, talk to people that have gardens, and gather as much information as you can!
